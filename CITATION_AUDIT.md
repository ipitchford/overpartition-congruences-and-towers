# Source comparison and bounded priority audit

Date: 12 October 2026. Retrospective publication-intake check, followed by source checking before the structural extensions. This record does not backdate the original discovery search.

1. [Wei, arXiv:2603.08510v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.08510v1): closing-section target congruences, modulo-17/23 starting forms, Proposition 2.4 and Remark 2.5 for real-character twists. Supplies the modulo-13 i=3 whole-class result. Our exact all-i classification is a reviewer-suggested extension of the checked operators, not attributed wholesale to Wei.
2. [Ryan, Sirolli, Villegas-Morales and Zheng, 2024](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11139-024-00953-z): Theorems 1.3 and 1.5 already provide square-prime Legendre-restricted families. The original cube-versus-square contrast was false and is removed. Compare actual primes, divisibility and additional modulo-8 restrictions instead. Modulo-7 and modulo-19 target arguments here are prime to the modulus prime.
3. [Aydin and Inam, 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.08461): Table 1, no. 43 gives eta(z)^2 eta(2z)^7 eta(4z)^2. This auxiliary eigenform is known. Its use does not by itself establish an earlier instance of the overpartition conclusion.
4. [Kong and Teo, 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.03280): equation (1.2), eta transformation convention used in the cusp appendix. Exact square-root phase conventions are stated in B.
5. Standard dependencies: Sturm's character-version theorem, Shimura Hecke operators, Gordon–Hughes–Newman eta conditions and Ligozat orders; precise bibliographic references appear in the manuscripts. Their use is not claimed as a new method.

Queries included overpartition Wei congruences 17 23; overpartition 13 powers of four congruence; and the named half-integral-weight eta eigenform paper. Relevant primary texts and supplied review comparisons were inspected. No exact earlier proof of the remaining target families was found in this bounded search. This is not an exhaustive literature search or priority guarantee.
